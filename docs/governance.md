# Governance

- **Name.** "Open Brand Exchange" (OBX) is a working name. It may change before 1.0 after a trademark search; the protocol, schemas and URLs will be renamed in one release if it does.
- **Initiator.** The specification was started by Photopilot, a Dutch company that makes product photography software. Until there are **three independent implementations** (from organisations that do not share ownership) that pass the protocol conformance profile, Photopilot's maintainers decide on changes. After that, a steering group with one seat per implementer (and at least one brand and one retailer representative) takes over, deciding by consensus and falling back to a two-thirds majority.
- **Neutrality.** Nothing in the specification may require a particular vendor, hosted service or proprietary component. Any organisation may implement it, host it for others, or build a competing product on it. Photopilot's implementation gets no special treatment in conformance.
- **Changes.** Proposals are pull requests to the `spec` repository with a short rationale ("RFC" label for anything beyond wording). Every normative change updates the OpenAPI files, schemas, prose, fixtures and changelog together, and must keep `npm run check` green. Maintainers leave substantive RFCs open for at least 14 days for comments.
- **Contributions** are accepted under the Developer Certificate of Origin (`Signed-off-by:` in commits); no contributor licence agreement.
- **Licences.** Specification text, schemas and fixtures: CC-BY-4.0. SDK, CLI and conformance suite: Apache-2.0. Reference nodes: AGPL-3.0. Website: CC-BY-4.0 content, MIT code.
- **Patents.** Photopilot commits not to assert any patent it holds or controls against an implementation of this specification, to the extent needed to implement it (in the spirit of the Open Web Foundation Final Specification Agreement). Contributors are asked to make the same commitment for their contributions.
- **Conformance claims.** "OBX-conformant" may be used by any implementation that passes the corresponding conformance profile of the claimed version; the claim names the profile(s), e.g. "OBX 0.1 protocol + ingest".
