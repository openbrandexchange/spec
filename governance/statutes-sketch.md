# Open Brand Exchange Foundation: sketch of the statutes

> **This is a sketch for a notary, not legal advice and not a deed.** It sets out what the founder wants the statutes of the foundation to achieve, in plain English, so that a Dutch civil-law notary can draft the real deed. Article numbers of the Dutch Civil Code (*Burgerlijk Wetboek*, BW) are given where the sketch relies on them; the notary checks them and every provision against current law. Where the sketch offers a choice, it is written as **[option A / option B]**. The Dutch version for the notary is [`statuten-schets.md`](statuten-schets.md); where the two differ, the notary's reading of the intent decides.

Status: draft of 2026-09-28, prepared for the founder, Richard Lindhout. Nothing here has legal effect until a notarial deed is executed.

## Article 1. Name and seat

1. The foundation is called **[Stichting Open Brand Exchange]**. It presents itself as **Open Brand Exchange Foundation**, in every language, without translating the name.
   *Note for the notary:* the statutory name must contain the word *stichting* (art. 2:286 BW). Please advise whether "Stichting Open Brand Exchange Foundation" or "Stichting Open Brand Exchange" with the trade name "Open Brand Exchange Foundation" is preferable.
2. It has its seat in **[municipality in the Netherlands]**.

## Article 2. Purpose

1. The purpose of the foundation is to develop, maintain, protect and promote **an open standard for the exchange of product content between brands and retailers** (the "Standard", currently called Open Brand Exchange), and to keep the Standard, its reference implementations and its conformance tests **freely available to everyone**, in the general interest of a fair and open market for product information.
2. The foundation pursues its purpose by, among other things:
   a. publishing the Standard under an open licence, and the software around it under open-source licences;
   b. holding and managing the copyrights, the trademark, the domain names and the online accounts of the project;
   c. maintaining a public conformance test suite and allowing everyone who passes it to say so on equal terms;
   d. keeping a public directory of brands that publish content under the Standard;
   e. working with brands, retailers, implementers and other standards bodies;
   f. all other lawful means that serve the purpose.
3. The foundation does not aim to make a profit. It may charge fees that cover its costs (for example for events or certification), provided the Standard itself stays free to read and implement.

## Article 3. No members, no profit distribution

1. The foundation has no members and no shareholders (art. 2:285 BW).
2. The foundation makes no distributions to its founder, to the members of its bodies or to anyone else, except distributions to others that have an idealistic or social purpose and serve the foundation's purpose (art. 2:285 paragraph 3 BW).
3. Board members and advisory board members are not paid for their membership. They may receive **reasonable compensation** for work actually done for the foundation, and reimbursement of costs actually made, on terms that the board sets in writing and publishes. A board member does not vote on his or her own compensation (see article 9).

## Article 4. Assets and asset lock

1. The assets of the foundation consist of: the copyrights and other intellectual property rights transferred to or created by it, the trademark "Open Brand Exchange" and related marks and logos, domain names (including `openbrandexchange.com`), online accounts (including the GitHub organisation `openbrandexchange`), donations, grants, subsidies, fees and other income.
2. **Asset lock.** The foundation's intellectual property rights, domain names and online accounts ("Core Assets") may only be used for the purpose in article 2. They may only be transferred, or exclusively licensed, to a legal entity that:
   a. does not aim to make a profit and makes no profit distributions;
   b. has a purpose that is the same as or very close to the purpose in article 2; and
   c. is bound by an asset lock at least as strict as this article.
3. A transfer or licence of a Core Asset is a fundamental decision (article 10), except the licences in paragraph 4.
4. The following licences are part of the ordinary course of business and need no fundamental decision, as long as they follow the policy the foundation has published:
   a. publishing the Standard under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 and the software under the open-source licence of each repository (Apache-2.0 or AGPL-3.0 at the time of this sketch), or a later version of the same licence;
   b. the non-exclusive trademark licence under the published trademark policy (article 11).
5. Licences already granted to the public cannot be revoked by the foundation; this article does not affect them.

## Article 5. The board

1. The foundation is governed by a board of at least **[one / three]** and at most **[seven]** natural persons. *Recommendation:* at least three once the founder has appointed them, so that the founder's position (article 6) and the conflict-of-interest rule (article 9) can work.
2. The **founder**, Richard Lindhout, is a board member and the **chair** for as long as he holds office. The board appoints a secretary and a treasurer from among its members; one person may hold more than one of these roles, but not the chair's.
3. **Appointment.** For as long as the founder is a board member, he alone appoints the other board members (a **personal right** of the founder: it cannot be transferred or inherited, and it ends when his board membership ends). After that, the board appoints new board members by co-optation, by a unanimous decision of the remaining board members.
4. Board members other than the founder are appointed for a term of **[four]** years and may be reappointed **[twice]**. The founder is appointed for an indefinite period.
5. When board seats are vacant, the remaining board members (or the only remaining one) stay a valid board. If the board cannot be completed, any interested party may ask the court to appoint board members (art. 2:299 BW).
6. At least **[half]** of the board members, and after the founder's departure a majority, must have no ties to any single company that implements the Standard commercially (see article 9 for what "ties" means).

## Article 6. The founder's position

1. The founder can only be **removed** as a board member or as chair by a **unanimous decision of all other board members**, taken in a meeting at which the founder was invited to be heard. The founder does not vote on his own removal.
2. This does not limit the power of the court to dismiss a board member under **art. 2:298 BW** (for example for neglect of duty or mismanagement); that power cannot be excluded by the statutes.
3. The founder's board membership also ends by his death, by his written resignation, or when he is placed under guardianship or his assets under administration.
4. When the founder's membership ends, his appointment right (article 5.3) and his chair's role end with it. The board then chooses a chair from among its members.

## Article 7. End of board membership (other board members)

A board member other than the founder stops being a board member:
a. at the end of his or her term, unless reappointed;
b. by written resignation;
c. by removal by the founder, for as long as the founder is a board member, after the board member has been heard; afterwards by a unanimous decision of the other board members;
d. by dismissal by the court (art. 2:298 BW);
e. by death, guardianship or administration of assets.

## Article 8. Board meetings and decisions

1. The board meets at least **[twice]** a year and whenever the chair or two board members ask. Meetings may be held by video; decisions may also be taken in writing (including e-mail) if all board members with a vote have agreed to that way of deciding.
2. Each board member has one vote. Unless these statutes require more, decisions are taken by a **simple majority** of the votes cast. **[If the votes are tied, the chair has the casting vote / the proposal is rejected.]**
3. The board keeps minutes of its decisions and publishes a summary of the decisions that concern the Standard, the licences or the trademark.
4. The board sets rules for its work in a board regulation, which may not conflict with these statutes.

## Article 9. Conflicts of interest

1. A board member does not take part in the deliberation and decision-making on a matter in which he or she has a direct or indirect personal interest that conflicts with the interest of the foundation (**art. 2:291 BW**).
2. In any case, a board member who is a director, employee, shareholder, owner or paid adviser of a company that implements the Standard commercially, or who is a close relative of such a person, does not vote on decisions that specifically affect that company, its products or its competitors. This includes decisions on conformance of that company's implementation, on the trademark licence to it, on complaints about it, and on payments to it. **Photopilot**, the company of the founder, is such a company; the rule applies to the founder too.
3. If as a result no decision can be taken by the board, the decision is taken by the **advisory board** (article 10), by a majority of its independent members.
4. Every board member reports a possible conflict to the chair (the chair to the other board members) before the matter is discussed. The report and the way it was handled are recorded in the minutes.

## Article 10. Advisory board and the double lock

1. The foundation has an **advisory board** of at least **[two]** and at most **[nine]** natural persons who are not board members.
2. **Independence.** At first, the advisory board consists only of **independent members**: persons who have no ties (as defined in article 9.2) to a company that implements the Standard commercially and who are not relatives of a board member. The board appoints them; while the founder is a board member, the founder's appointment of an independent member needs the consent of the advisory board members already in office (if any).
3. **Seats for the field.** Once the Standard is in real use, the board adds seats for **brands** (companies that publish content under the Standard) and for **implementers** (companies or projects that implement it), each group choosing its own representatives in a way the board lays down in a regulation. Independent members always keep at least **half** of the seats.
4. The advisory board advises the board, on request or on its own initiative, and approves fundamental decisions (paragraph 5). Its members serve for **[four]** years and may be reappointed.
5. **Fundamental decisions (double lock).** The following decisions require **a unanimous decision of the board** (all board members in office; board members excluded under article 9 do not count) **and the prior written approval of the advisory board**, given by a majority of at least **two thirds** of its members, including a majority of its independent members:
   a. amending these statutes (article 13);
   b. transferring, pledging or otherwise encumbering a Core Asset, or granting an exclusive licence or any licence outside the published policy (article 4.4) for the trademark, a domain name, copyrights or other intellectual property;
   c. changing the licence of the Standard or of the software, or the trademark policy;
   d. a legal merger or demerger (article 14);
   e. dissolution (article 15);
   f. applying for bankruptcy or suspension of payments.
6. As long as the advisory board has fewer than **[two]** members, no fundamental decision can be taken, except appointing advisory board members.
7. A fundamental decision taken without the required approval is void as far as the law allows; the board cannot represent the foundation for it (see article 12.2).

## Article 11. The trademark and conformance

1. The foundation registers and maintains the trademark "Open Brand Exchange" (and, as the board decides, "OBX" and the logo), starting with a Benelux registration at the Benelux Office for Intellectual Property (BOIP).
2. The foundation publishes a **trademark policy** under which **every implementer, on the same terms**, may use the name to state that its product implements or works with the Standard, and may use a conformance label for an implementation that passes the public conformance test suite for the claimed version and profile.
3. The licence is non-exclusive and free of charge. The only technical condition is conformance. No implementer, the founder's company included, receives an exclusive licence or better terms.
4. The foundation may refuse or end a licence only on grounds that apply equally to everyone (for example failing conformance, or misleading use), after hearing the licensee.

## Article 12. Representation

1. The board represents the foundation. The foundation is also represented by **[the chair together with one other board member / two board members acting jointly]**.
2. The board may not represent the foundation for a fundamental decision (article 10.5) unless the decision was taken with the double lock.
3. The board is authorised to enter into agreements for the acquisition, disposal or encumbrance of registered property and agreements in which the foundation binds itself as guarantor or joint debtor, **[only with the approval of the advisory board]**.

## Article 13. Amendment of the statutes

1. The board may amend these statutes (art. 2:293 BW), but only as a fundamental decision (article 10.5).
2. **Articles 2 (purpose), 3 (no profit distribution), 4 (asset lock), 10 (double lock) and 15.3 (destination of the surplus)** may not be amended in a way that would allow profit distribution, the use of the Core Assets for another purpose, or their transfer to an entity that does not meet article 4.2.
3. An amendment takes effect only once it has been laid down in a notarial deed. Every board member is authorised to have the deed executed.

## Article 14. Merger and demerger

A legal merger or demerger is a fundamental decision (article 10.5) and is only allowed with a legal entity that meets the requirements of article 4.2.

## Article 15. Dissolution and liquidation

1. The foundation may be dissolved by a fundamental decision (article 10.5). It is also dissolved in the other cases the law provides.
2. The board members act as liquidators, unless the decision appoints others.
3. Any **surplus** after liquidation goes to a Dutch or foreign legal entity that does not aim to make a profit, makes no profit distributions and has a purpose that is the same as or very close to the purpose of the foundation, **preferably one that will continue to maintain the Standard**. The Core Assets go to that entity with the obligation to keep the Standard freely available.
4. After the liquidation the books and records are kept for the legally required period by a person the liquidators designate.

## Article 16. Absence and inability

If a board member is absent or unable to act (*ontstentenis of belet*), the remaining board members are temporarily in charge. If all board members are absent or unable to act, **[the chair of the advisory board / a person designated by the board in advance in writing]** is temporarily in charge and appoints new board members as soon as possible. *Note for the notary:* the WBTR (2021) requires a provision on this point.

## Article 17. Financial year, accounts and transparency

1. The financial year is the calendar year.
2. The board keeps records of the foundation's financial position (art. 2:10 BW) and draws up a balance sheet and a statement of income and expenditure within six months after the end of each financial year.
3. The board publishes each year a short report on the Standard, the implementations, the finances (in summary) and the compensation paid to board members.

## Article 18. Regulations and cases not provided for

The board may lay down regulations (for its own work, the advisory board, the trademark policy, the technical process) that do not conflict with the law or these statutes. In cases not provided for, the board decides, in the spirit of articles 2 and 4.

## Transitional provision

At incorporation, the founder is the only board member and the chair. He appoints further board members and the first independent advisory board members as soon as possible, and in any case **[within twelve months]**. Until the advisory board has at least **[two]** members, the foundation takes no fundamental decisions (article 10.6).

## Transfer of the project to the foundation (steps after incorporation)

These are the practical steps; each needs the founder's signature and some need the notary.

1. **Incorporation.** Notarial deed of incorporation; registration in the Trade Register of the Chamber of Commerce (KvK), including the board members and the UBO registration; bank account in the foundation's name.
2. **Copyright.** A **deed of transfer of copyright** (a written deed is required, art. 2 of the Dutch Copyright Act, *Auteurswet*) from Richard Lindhout to the foundation, covering the specification, schemas, fixtures, SDK, CLI, conformance suite, admin UI, reference portals, website texts and logo, listing the repositories and the commit at the moment of transfer, and including future work he contributes.
   *Check first:* whether any of it was made by employees of, or on behalf of, a company (for example Photopilot) that would own the copyright as employer (art. 7 *Auteurswet*); if so, that company co-signs the deed. Contributions of third parties are not transferred; they stay available under the repository licences (see [governance.md](../docs/governance.md#contributions)).
3. **Trademark.** File the word mark "Open Brand Exchange" at BOIP (Benelux), [classes 9, 35 and 42], **in the foundation's name** once it exists. If it was filed earlier in the founder's name, sign a transfer and have BOIP record it. An EU trademark (EUIPO) can follow later.
4. **Domain.** Change the registrant of `openbrandexchange.com` (and any other domains) to the foundation; move DNS, e-mail routing for `hello@` and `security@`, and the Cloudflare account to an account of the foundation with at least two administrators.
5. **GitHub.** Make the foundation the owner of the `openbrandexchange` organisation: at least two owners appointed by the board, two-factor authentication required, billing in the foundation's name. Repositories stay where they are.
6. **Texts.** Replace "Richard Lindhout" and "(in formation)" in the NOTICE files, copyright lines, governance pages and the website imprint by "Open Brand Exchange Foundation", in one change per repository.
7. **Commitments.** The foundation adopts the patent commitment and the trademark policy, and publishes the first board and advisory board composition.
